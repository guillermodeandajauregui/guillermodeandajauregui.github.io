# Systems Pharmacology

## Drugs don't act in isolation

Drugs are designed to produce therapeutic effects, but their actions extend beyond individual molecular targets. They interact with interconnected biological pathways, regulatory networks, and physiological systems.

These interactions can produce beneficial responses, unexpected therapeutic opportunities, and adverse drug reactions.

**Understanding what a drug does requires understanding the system it perturbs.**

Systems pharmacology brings together pharmacology, network science, computational biology, and data science to investigate these effects from a broader perspective.

## Understanding both sides of drug action

Our research explores how drugs interact with biological systems, how their effects propagate through molecular networks, and how these interactions can inform drug discovery, repurposing, and combination therapies.

A particular focus of our work is the study of adverse drug reactions.

We investigate how molecular mechanisms, drug combinations, chemical properties, and real-world pharmacovigilance data can help us characterize and anticipate unwanted drug effects.

Our approaches include network-based modeling, computational analysis of molecular perturbations, and machine learning methods applied to pharmacological and biomedical data.

Ultimately, we are interested in a fundamental challenge of pharmacology: understanding not only why treatments work, but also why they sometimes fail or cause harm.
