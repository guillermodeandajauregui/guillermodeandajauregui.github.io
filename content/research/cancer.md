# Cancer, Networks & Artificial Intelligence

## Understanding cancer as a complex disease

Cancer is not a single disease with a single origin or a predictable course. Tumors can emerge through different molecular mechanisms, develop distinct biological characteristics, and respond differently to treatment. Even patients with seemingly similar diagnoses may experience very different outcomes.

Understanding this diversity requires looking beyond individual genes, mutations, or molecular markers.

**Complex diseases call for systems-level thinking.**

Network science provides a framework for studying biological systems through their interactions, organization, and collective behavior. Instead of treating genes, proteins, and regulatory mechanisms as isolated components, we investigate how they work together, how their relationships change in disease, and what those changes reveal about cancer biology.

Our research has explored the organization of gene co-expression networks, the regulatory roles of microRNAs, and the use of molecular network signatures to investigate potential therapeutic strategies.

## New opportunities through AI

Artificial intelligence is opening new opportunities to investigate biological complexity.

Machine learning allows us to explore large, heterogeneous datasets, identify patterns across different biological scales, and integrate information that traditional approaches often analyze separately.

We are particularly interested in combining network-based representations with artificial intelligence to advance our understanding of cancer and develop computational approaches with potential clinical applications.

Our current work includes the development of multimodal AI models integrating medical imaging, transcriptomic profiles, and clinical information, with breast cancer as an initial application.

Our multimodal breast cancer initiative received recognition and support from *Alianza Médica para la Salud (AMSA)*, including the 2026 AMSA research award.
