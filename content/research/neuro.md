# Neurodegeneration, Aging & Multiomics

## Understanding nervous systems through complexity

Nervous systems are remarkable examples of biological complexity. Their functions emerge from interactions among cells, molecular pathways, and regulatory processes operating across multiple spatial and temporal scales.

Neurodegenerative diseases disrupt these interconnected systems. As populations age, conditions such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease represent an increasingly important challenge for public health.

Understanding how nervous systems age, why neurodegenerative diseases emerge, and why they affect individuals differently requires more than studying isolated molecular mechanisms.

**We need to understand how biological systems change, reorganize, and sometimes fail.**

## From big data to biological understanding

Large international research efforts are generating unprecedented amounts of molecular, clinical, and multiomic data on aging and neurodegeneration.

These resources provide opportunities to investigate disease mechanisms across biological scales. But generating data is only part of the challenge. Making sense of it requires computational methods, systems-level perspectives, and teams bringing together expertise from different disciplines.

Our research combines transcriptomics, network science, and computational approaches to investigate the molecular organization of neurodegenerative diseases.

Our work includes exploring the ROSMAP resource and its opportunities for systems-level research, studying gene co-expression networks in Alzheimer's disease, comparing early- and late-onset forms, and investigating the reorganization of molecular pathways during neurodegeneration.

Our interest in nervous systems also extends beyond neurodegeneration. In collaborative research on suicide neurobiology, we have used transcription factor networks to investigate differences in gene regulation across psychiatric conditions, illustrating how network-based approaches can contribute to understanding diverse problems in neuroscience.

**Complex problems need more than big datasets. They need great teams.**
