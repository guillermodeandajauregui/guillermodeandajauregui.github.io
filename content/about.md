I am Guillermo de Anda Jáuregui, a biomedical scientist working at the intersection of network science, complex systems, and computational biomedicine.

I trained as a Pharmaceutical Biological Chemist (QFB) at the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM), where I also earned my PhD in Biomedical Sciences. I subsequently conducted postdoctoral research at the University of North Dakota School of Medicine and Health Sciences.

I am a researcher in the *Investigadoras e Investigadores por México* program of SECIHTI, currently working at the National Institute of Genomic Medicine (INMEGEN). I also collaborate with the research community at UNAM's Center for Complexity Sciences (C3).

I am a Level II member of Mexico's National System of Researchers (SNII) and a Joven Académico of the Mexican Academy of Sciences (AMC).

Beyond my formal academic work, I am an enthusiastic scientific programmer, particularly in R.

I enjoy building computational tools, exploring unconventional approaches to difficult problems, and finding ways to make sophisticated methods accessible and reusable.

If I ain't hacking or sciencing, you'll probably find me in the mosh pit.
